The patent example reports particles from 0.09 to 0.70 micrometres across. A micrometre is one thousandth of a millimetre. The game draws the particles much larger so you can see them.

What are the anode and cathode?

The anode is the + plate in this tank. It supplies copper for the grains.

The cathode is the − plate in this tank. Hydrogen bubbles form there.

Why turn on the power?

Electric current is the movement of electric charge. The power supply keeps charge moving and drives changes at both plates. Copper leaves one plate and hydrogen bubbles form at the other.

Switch off the power to see what changes. The plates stop supplying copper and making new bubbles. The grains already in the tank stay at the bottom.

What is in the tank?

The tank holds water with dissolved table salt. Salt splits into charged pieces called ions. They can move through the water and carry charge between the plates.

What does the tube do?
  1. The hood collects hydrogen above the right plate.
  2. The tube carries the gas to the heated chamber.
  3. The reactor uses the gas to turn copper oxide grains into copper. The grains arrive separately after filtering, rinsing and drying.
What else is in the patent diagram?

The tank has several pairs of copper plates. Hoods above the − plates collect gas. An outlet at the bottom lets the grains out. The reactor holds a powder tray. Excess gas can bubble through a water vessel after the reactor.

The patent text describes filtering, rinsing and drying. The game shows those steps at a single station. See Figure 1 of the patent.

Need a hint?

Say “What is an ion?” or “Where does the powder form?” Your browser may send audio to a speech recognition service. The game stores no audio or transcript.

    How it works

    Lab words
    Current
    The movement of electric charge. In this tank, it drives changes at the plates.
    Anode and cathode
    The names of the two plates. Here, the anode supplies copper and the cathode is where hydrogen bubbles appear.
    Copper oxide
    A solid made of copper joined to oxygen. It forms the grains you collect from the tank.
    Hydrogen
    The gas in the bubbles. A tube carries it to the reactor.
    Filter
    A barrier with tiny openings. Liquid passes through and solid grains stay behind.
    Reactor
    A chamber where materials change. Here, hydrogen takes oxygen from the grains to make water, leaving copper behind.
